Pattern

Empty and error states

What a screen shows when there is nothing yet, and when something has gone wrong.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stable
  • Overview
  • When to use what
  • Structure
  • States
  • Responsive behaviour
  • Accessibility
  • Content
  • Do and don’t
  • Sources

Overview

Most screens can never be empty: there are always five kids, always a setup, always a card. Where one can, it says what is missing and offers the one thing to do.

When to use what

CaseWhat shows
A new child on HomeNo kid chip; a line says there are no names and no sign-up; Play leads to Pick Your Kid
A host alone in the LobbySeven empty desks, a disabled Start and “Waiting for at least one friend…”
Room with no lettersFour empty boxes and a disabled Join
The backend cannot be reachedAn alert with Try again, and Play solo still works
Too many tries“Wait a moment”, and the control is disabled
A lost connection in a gameReconnecting for 30 seconds, then the game is lost and Home is the way on
An address that leads nowhereA Notice with Home
The game itself breaksA notice with Try again and Home

Structure

We couldn’t make your room

Something went wrong on our side. Try again, or play solo while you wait.

An error keeps the screen and offers a way on

States

StateRule
ErrorThe screen stays; what the child chose stays chosen
OfflineA banner; everything that needs no network still works
Unknown error codeA generic message; the backend’s detail is never shown

Responsive behaviour

Messages fill the column they are in. A notice is centred at every width.

Accessibility

  • An error is an alert in words, with an icon as well as a colour.
  • Focus stays where it was; the alert is read without moving it.

Content

  • Formula: what happened + what to do. “That game already started. Ask for the next one!”
  • Never blame: “That wasn’t you.”
