Pattern

Navigation and screen structure

Ten screens in a line. A child is playing within 20 seconds and five choices.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stable
  • Overview
  • When to use what
  • Structure
  • States
  • Responsive behaviour

Overview

There is no menu. Each screen has one way on and Back. Home, Pick Your Kid, Solo Or Friends, then either Game Setup (solo) or Room and Lobby (friends), Mic Check when needed, and the game: Duel, World Map, Podium.

When to use what

Kind of screenStructure
Menu screensTop bar with Back and the title; a body; the way on at the end
HomeNo top bar: the classroom, the title, Play, and two corner buttons
Duel and World MapFill the window: a HUD, a stage and a footer
PodiumNo top bar: the headline is the h1; Play again and Home

Structure

Who’s playing?

Back, and the screen’s name

States

MomentRule
BackOpens the screen before, with what was chosen still chosen
A returning childHome’s Play skips Pick Your Kid; the kid chip offers “Change kid”
Mic CheckShown once a visit, and skipped when the browser cannot listen
In a duelPause (solo) or Leave (friends) is the only way out, and both ask first
An address for a game you are not inOpens Home

Responsive behaviour

The structure is the same at every width; only columns change. See Responsive.

Accessibility

  • Every screen has a skip link, one <header> where there is a top bar, one <main> and one <h1>.
  • Going somewhere is a link; doing something is a button.
  • The page title names the screen.

Content

  • Screen titles: Pick your kid · Who’s playing? · Join a room · Room · Set up your game · Mic check · World map.
