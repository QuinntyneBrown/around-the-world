Foundations

ColorLight sky blue backgrounds, white panels and navy text, with one bright colour for each kid.TypographyFredoka for numbers, names and buttons; Nunito for sentences.SpacingA 4px scale from 0 to 96px.LayoutFive sizes from extra small to extra large, a page that is one column until 992px, and two screens that fill the window and never scroll.ElevationThings rise in two ways: a soft shadow for what floats, and a solid ledge for what you can press.ShapeEverything is round.MotionThe hop is the reward and lasts about a second.IconographyRound line icons on a 24-unit grid with a 2.5px stroke, in the colour of the text beside them.ThemingThree tiers of tokens, two themes, one file that holds every value.ResponsiveEvery screen works from the narrowest phone to the widest desktop, and Duel fills the screen at all of them.AccessibilityWCAG 2.2 AA, for children: playable by touch and by keyboard, never by colour alone.ContentShort, warm and plain: the words a ten-year-old would say across a classroom.

Components

Actions

Toy buttonThe chunky button that sits on a solid base and sinks when pressed.Icon buttonA square toy button that holds one icon: Back, sound, Settings, Pause, Leave, copy.Text buttonA quiet action in words: Change, Easy, All, “Too noisy? Tap answers instead”, “Use number pad”.Kid chipThe kid and nickname last picked, on Home, and the way to change them.

Navigation

Top barThe slim header on every menu screen: Back, the screen’s title, and an optional right slot.Skip linkThe first thing Tab reaches on every screen: “Skip to the game”.

Layout

Screen and stackThe frame every screen sits in, and the two helpers that space what is inside it.

Containers

PanelA white surface with a soft edge that groups one decision: the nickname, the game’s setup, a kind of choice.DialogA question over the screen that must be answered before going on: Paused, Leave the game?, Settings.

The cast

PortraitA kid’s picture on their own colour.Kid tagA kid’s name on their colour: the label that floats over them on the World Map.BadgeA small word on a picture: Host.NicknameThe generated game name and the button that rolls another.

Data display

PillA rounded line of text that floats over the classroom: “Italy · desk 5 of 12”.SummaryThe game’s setup as three lines in the Lobby: the maths, the tour and how answers are given.

Choices

Kid pickerThe roster of five kids on Pick Your Kid.Mode cardOne of the two ways to play on Solo Or Friends: Just me, or With friends.Toggle chipA kind of maths that is on or off: Times, Plus, Minus, Divide.Table keyOne times table, from ×2 to ×12, that is on or off.ChoiceOne of three tour lengths: 6, 12 or 24 desks.Segmented choiceThree card speeds in one track: Chill, Normal, Lightning.SwitchA setting that is on or off at once: Sound, and Answer by shouting.

Rooms

Code lettersFour letters in boxes: a picture of what a room code looks like.Code fieldThe four boxes a child types a room’s code into.Room codeThe room’s four letters in the largest type on the Lobby, with a button that copies the link.Player cardOne desk in the Lobby: a player’s kid and nickname, or an empty desk.

Feedback

Field messageThe line under the code field that says what is happening or what went wrong.Status overlayA card over a dimmed Duel while the game cannot go on: Reconnecting, or the game is lost.Alert and bannerA message that stays in the page: something could not be done, or something to know before going on.ToastA short message in the corner of the Lobby about something that just happened: a friend joined, the link was copied.SpinnerA small turning ring inside a busy button.SkeletonGrey shapes in the layout of what is arriving.NoticeA whole screen with one message and one way on: an address that leads nowhere, or the game tripping over.

Voice

ListenerMilo and Bolt in a circle on Mic Check, with a badge that says whether the microphone hears.Level meterBars that move with the child’s voice, on Mic Check and inside “Shout it!”.

Duel

Duel HUDWho is duelling: the traveller and the desk holder, each with their kid, name and role, over the timer bar.Timer barThe bar that drains over a flash card’s time, in the traveller’s colour.World ringA tiny map of every desk round the room, with a dot for each kid, on Duel.Flash cardThe sum, and under it how to answer or what just happened.Number padTwelve keys for answering by tapping: the digits, Delete and Go.Stamp cardThe passport stamp that lands when a duel is won: the next country, its landmark, and the hop.StickerThe six cheers a watching player can send, and how one shows when it arrives.

World Map

World Map roomThe classroom seen from above: the ring of desks, the kids at them, the traveller on the path, and the country plaques.Map messageThe line that drops in over World Map when a duel is decided: who walks on, or who sits down.StandingsThe five kids in order of how far they have travelled, under the World Map.

Podium

PodiumThe top three on steps of their own colour, with a headline that says who went round the world.PassportThe record of countries reached: a stamp for each, a placeholder for the rest, and the child’s place.

Patterns

Choices and the code fieldHow a child sets things up: toggles and choices that take effect at once, and the one field in the game.Feedback and loadingWhich kind of answer the game gives to what just happened, and what shows while something arrives.Empty and error statesWhat a screen shows when there is nothing yet, and when something has gone wrong.Navigation and screen structureTen screens in a line.Dialogs and overlaysWhen the game stops to ask, and when it stops because it must.Lists: players, standings and stampsThe three collections in the game.NotificationsThe four kinds of passing message, and which one a moment gets.Content and toneHow the game speaks at each moment of a tour.

How to use

  • Link tokens/tokens.css, then assets/components.css. The mocks do exactly this.
  • Use the classes as written. A component is a BEM block; a state a screen reader must know is an attribute (aria-pressed, aria-current, aria-invalid, aria-busy, disabled) and is styled through it.
  • In the Angular workspace each block becomes one component in the components library with its own encapsulated stylesheet; global styles hold only the tokens, the font faces and the reset.
  • Other tools read tokens/tokens.json.
  • A new screen is drawn in docs/mocks first; a new component is added here in the same change.
