# L1 - High-Level Requirements

Around the World is the classroom flash-card game as a web game for children aged 10 to 12.
These requirements are derived from the **Around The World** prototype in Magic Patterns (the
frames Home, Pick Your Kid, Solo Or Friends, Room, Lobby, Game Setup, Mic Check, Duel, World Map
and Podium), the *Around The World Game Plan* and `AGENTS.md`. Each L1 is detailed in
[`L2.md`](L2.md).

## L1-001: The tour and its duels
The game must play the classroom game: a traveller duels a desk holder over one flash card, the
first correct answer wins, the winner travels on and the loser sits, and the first player all
the way round the ring of desks wins the tour.

## L1-002: Flash cards
The game must make flash cards from the kinds of maths chosen for the game (times tables from 2
to 12, plus, minus and divide), each with one whole-number answer, and never a fraction or a
word problem.

## L1-003: Answering by voice or number pad
A child must answer by shouting the number into the device, with the number pad as the fallback
whenever speech recognition or the microphone is not available, refused or not wanted.

## L1-004: A kid and a nickname, with no account
A child must pick one of the five kids and receive a generated nickname, both kept on the
device, without an account, a sign-in, a real name or anything typed about themselves.

## L1-005: Game setup
The game must be set up once (the kinds of maths, the times tables, the tour length and the card
speed), with defaults that start a game with no changes.

## L1-006: A solo game against classmates
A child must be able to play alone, on the device, against classmates who are agents: each has
its own skill, characteristics and preferred kinds of maths, so that the class is diverse. Where
the browser provides an on-device language model (Gemini Nano in Chrome), it drives the
classmates; where it does not, the game plays the same without it.

## L1-007: Rooms and the lobby
Children must be able to play together in a room of up to 8, made by a host and joined with a
4-letter code read aloud, and wait in a lobby until the host starts the game.

## L1-008: A game with friends in real time
A game with friends must be played in real time, with the server deciding who answered first,
every player seeing the same game, a player who is not in the duel watching it, and a player who
loses their connection able to return.

## L1-009: The screens and the way through them
The game must lead a child through Home, Pick Your Kid, Solo Or Friends, Room, Lobby, Game
Setup, Mic Check, Duel, World Map and Podium, and have a child playing within 20 seconds of
opening it.

## L1-010: The 3D classroom and its cast
The game must show the classroom as a 3D ring of desks, each a country with its landmark, and
the cast as animated 3D characters, drawn smoothly on low-power devices.

## L1-011: Motion and sound
The game must reward a hop with motion and sound, keep losing gentle, let every sound be muted
and replace motion with quick fades when reduced motion is asked for.

## L1-012: Safe for children
The game must be safe for children: no accounts, no real names, no free-text chat, no adverts or
purchases, nothing a child types or says leaving the device as text or sound, and nothing a
language model writes shown to a child.

## L1-013: Security
The backend must accept only what a room's own player may do, validate everything it receives,
limit the rate of anything open to anyone, and hold no personal data.

## L1-014: Performance, loading and memory
The game must open fast, stay within a small initial bundle, load heavy parts only when needed,
release what it acquires and stay as smooth on the last desk of a 24-desk tour as on the first.

## L1-015: Responsive at every width
Every screen must work from the narrowest phone to the widest desktop: extra small (<576px),
small (>=576px), medium (>=768px), large (>=992px) and extra large (>=1200px).

## L1-016: Accessibility
Every screen must meet WCAG 2.2 AA: playable by touch and by keyboard, with touch targets of at
least 44 x 44 px, and never with colour as the only carrier of a state.

## L1-017: Operation and observability
The backend must run as a .NET service whose SignalR connections scale out through Azure SignalR
Service, must be observable through OpenTelemetry without recording anything about a child, and
must fail in ways a screen can explain.
