# L2 - Detailed Requirements

Each requirement traces to one L1 in [`L1.md`](L1.md). Every acceptance test names the L2
requirements it covers in a header comment (`Traces to: L2-001, L2-003`).

The prototype's names and numbers (the nicknames, the countries, the code `BOLT`) are the cast,
not rules. The mocks in `docs/mocks/` are the source of truth for the interface, and these
requirements follow them. A value marked **(to confirm)** is not settled by the mocks, or is one
of their open questions, and is an assumption until it is settled.

## L2-001: A duel is won by the first correct answer
**Traces to:** L1-001

A duel is between the traveller and the desk holder of the desk the traveller stands beside,
over one flash card. The first correct answer wins the duel.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a duel over `6 × 4`, when the traveller answers 24 before the desk holder answers, then the traveller wins the duel.
2. Given a duel over `6 × 4`, when the desk holder answers 24 before the traveller answers, then the desk holder wins the duel.
3. Given a duel, when a player who is neither the traveller nor the desk holder sends an answer, then the answer is ignored and the duel is unchanged.
4. Given a duel that has been won, when the other player then answers correctly, then the result is unchanged.

---

## L2-002: The traveller who wins hops on
**Traces to:** L1-001

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given the traveller wins a duel at desk 5 of 12, when the duel ends, then the traveller hops to desk 6 and duels its desk holder, and the beaten desk holder keeps their desk.
2. Given the traveller wins a duel, when the hop ends, then the traveller's passport holds a stamp for the country of the desk reached.
3. Given the first traveller of a tour stands at desk 5 of 12 with a distance travelled of 4, when they hop to desk 6, then their distance travelled is 5.
4. Given the next desk is an empty desk, when the traveller hops, then they pass it and stop at the desk after it, and their distance travelled goes up by 1.

---

## L2-003: The desk holder who wins becomes the traveller
**Traces to:** L1-001

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given the desk holder wins a duel, when the duel ends, then the beaten traveller sits down at that desk and becomes its desk holder.
2. Given the desk holder wins a duel, when the duel ends, then the former desk holder is the traveller and next duels the holder of the next desk.
3. Given a desk holder has just become the traveller, when they walk on to the next desk, then their distance travelled is 1 if it was 0 and is otherwise what it already was; each later hop adds 1, and a distance travelled never goes down or starts again.
4. Given a desk holder has just become the traveller, when they walk on to the next desk, then their passport receives no stamp for it; a passport holds a stamp only for a desk reached by a hop (L2-002 criterion 2) and, for the first traveller of a tour, desk 1 (L2-007 criterion 7), and a desk holder's own desk is not one.

---

## L2-004: A wrong answer freezes the player for one second
**Traces to:** L1-001

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a duel, when a player gives a wrong answer, then that player is frozen for 1 second and every answer they give while frozen is ignored.
2. Given a player is frozen, when 1 second has passed on the clock, then the player can answer the same flash card again.
3. Given the traveller is frozen, when the desk holder answers correctly, then the desk holder wins the duel.
4. Given a wrong answer, when the player is frozen, then the Duel screen says so in words ("Frozen for a sec…"), the number pad is disabled, and the rival is not frozen.
5. Given a wrong answer, when it is given, then the flash card shakes once, is outlined, and names the answer given ("Not {answer}! Frozen for a sec…").

---

## L2-005: A flash card that nobody answers in time
**Traces to:** L1-001

Each flash card has a time to answer, set by the card speed: Chill 10 seconds, Normal 6 seconds,
Lightning 3 seconds. The timer bar drains over that time in the traveller's colour.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given card speed Normal, when a flash card appears, then the timer bar empties in 6 seconds (10 for Chill, 3 for Lightning).
2. Given neither player has answered correctly, when the card's time runs out, then nobody hops, the flash card says "Time's up! It was {answer}. Here comes a new card…" for 2 seconds, and the same two players then duel over a new flash card with nobody frozen (ADR 0020).

---

## L2-006: How a tour ends
**Traces to:** L1-001

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a tour of 12 desks, when a traveller hops past the last desk and so has gone all the way round, then the tour ends and that player wins.
2. Given a tour whose time limit has passed with nobody all the way round, when the current duel ends, then the tour ends and the player who travelled furthest wins. The time limit is 3 minutes for a tour of 6 desks, 6 minutes for 12 and 12 minutes for 24, counted in game time; no clock for it is shown during a duel, as the mocks draw none (ADR 0024). The current duel is the one being played, the child's or two classmates'; a flash card nobody answers does not end it (L2-005 criterion 2).
3. Given two players have travelled equally far when time runs out, when the tour ends, then the one who reached that distance first is placed higher.
4. Given a tour has ended, when the result is shown, then every player has a place from 1st to last by distance travelled.
5. Given any player, whichever desk they held when they first became the traveller, when their distance travelled equals the tour length, then they have gone all the way round.
6. Given two players have travelled equally far, whichever way the tour ends, when they are placed, then the one who reached that distance first is placed higher, and players level since the start of the tour keep the order of their desks; no place is shared (ADR 0024).

---

## L2-007: The class at the start of a tour
**Traces to:** L1-001

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a tour of 6, 12 or 24 desks, when it starts, then the ring has that many desks, each a different country with its landmark.
2. Given a tour starts, when the first duel appears, then one player is the traveller, their own desk is empty and every other player is a desk holder.
3. Given a solo game, when a tour starts, then the device's player is the traveller.
4. Given a tour of 6, 12 or 24 desks, when it starts, then the desks are numbered from 1 to the tour length, desk 1 is the first traveller's own desk and is the one empty desk among them, the traveller's distance travelled is 1 and the first duel is at desk 2.
5. Given a game with friends with fewer players than desks, when a tour starts, then classmates hold the desks no player holds, so that exactly one desk is empty.
6. Given any moment of a tour, when the ring is inspected, then exactly one player is the traveller and exactly one desk is empty, apart from the desk of each player removed from a game with friends (L2-029 criterion 4), which stays empty.
7. Given a tour starts, when the first traveller's passport is inspected, then it holds the stamp of desk 1, the desk the tour starts from, as their distance travelled of 1 counts it (criterion 4, ADR 0024).

---

## L2-008: Flash cards from the chosen maths
**Traces to:** L1-002

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given only Times is on with tables 2, 3, 4, 5 and 10, when a flash card is made, then it is `a × b` where one factor is a chosen table and the other is from 1 to 12.
2. Given several kinds of maths are on, when 100 flash cards are made, then every card is of a chosen kind and each chosen kind appears.
3. Given Divide is on, when a flash card is made, then it divides exactly, with a divisor that is never 0.
4. Given Minus is on, when a flash card is made, then the answer is never negative.
5. Given any setup, when a flash card is made, then its answer is a whole number from 0 to 999 and the card is never a fraction or a word problem.
6. Given a duel has just ended, when the next flash card is made, then it is not the same card as the last one.

---

## L2-009: Answering by voice
**Traces to:** L1-003

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given voice is the way of answering and a flash card `6 × 4` is showing, when the child says "twenty-four", then the answer 24 is given.
2. Given voice is the way of answering, when the child says something that is not a number, then no answer is given and the player is not frozen.
3. Given the child says a wrong number, when it is recognized, then the wrong answer is given and the player is frozen (L2-004).
4. Given voice is the way of answering, when a flash card is showing, then the Duel screen shows that the microphone is listening ("Shout it!").
5. Given the player is watching a duel, frozen, or not on the Duel screen, when the child speaks, then no answer is given and speech recognition is not running.

---

## L2-010: Answering with the number pad
**Traces to:** L1-003

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given the number pad is the way of answering, when the child presses 2, 4 and Go, then the answer 24 is given and the digits pressed show on the flash card as they are pressed.
2. Given three digits have been entered, when a fourth is pressed, then it is ignored.
3. Given digits have been entered, when Delete is pressed, then the last digit is removed.
4. Given no digit has been entered, when Go is pressed, then no answer is given.
5. Given a keyboard, when the child types digits, Backspace and Enter, then they act as the pad's digits, Delete and Go.
6. Given the player is frozen, when a key is pressed, then nothing is entered.
7. Given digits were entered and the answer was wrong, when the freeze ends, then the entry is empty.

---

## L2-011: Switching between voice and the number pad
**Traces to:** L1-003

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a duel answered by voice, when the child chooses "Use number pad", then the number pad shows and speech recognition stops.
2. Given a duel answered with the number pad and a working microphone, when the child chooses "Shout instead", then the pad hides and speech recognition starts.
3. Given the browser has no speech recognition, when the child reaches a duel, then the number pad is the way of answering and "Shout instead" is not offered.
4. Given speech recognition fails during a duel, when the failure is reported, then the number pad shows for that duel without losing the flash card, the Duel screen says so ("Bolt stopped hearing, so tap your answer for this one.") and "Shout instead" is not offered for that duel.
5. Given a duel the player is in, when a flash card is showing or the player is frozen, then the switch ("Use number pad" or "Shout instead") is offered, except as criteria 3 and 4 say; once the duel is decided, or while the player is watching, it is not.
6. Given the microphone is refused, on Mic Check or when "Shout instead" first asks for it, when the next duel starts in the same visit, then the number pad is the way of answering and "Shout instead" is not offered; after a failure of speech recognition during a duel, the next duel starts with the way of answering kept on the device (docs/adr/0025-answering-by-voice.md).

---

## L2-012: Mic Check
**Traces to:** L1-003

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a child who has not passed Mic Check starts a game, when the game is about to begin, then Mic Check asks to turn on the microphone and says that their voice is never saved or sent to anyone.
2. Given the child chooses "Turn on mic" and allows it, when they shout "Ready!", then Mic Check shows that it heard them and "Let's go!" starts the game with voice.
3. Given the child refuses the microphone, when the refusal is reported, then Mic Check says the microphone is off and offers "Use number pad", which starts the game with the pad.
4. Given Mic Check is asking or listening, when the child chooses "Too noisy? Tap answers instead", then the game starts with the number pad.
5. Given the browser has no speech recognition, when a game is about to begin, then Mic Check is skipped and the game starts with the number pad.
6. Given a child has passed Mic Check or chosen the number pad in this visit, when they play again, then Mic Check is not shown again.
7. Given the microphone has been refused, when Mic Check says so, then "Use number pad" is the only choice it offers besides Back, and it tells the child to ask a grown-up to allow the microphone in the browser.
8. Given Mic Check is shown before a solo game, when the child leaves it for Duel, then the first flash card appears then and its time starts then.
9. Given a game with friends, when the host starts the game, then each player who needs Mic Check (criteria 1, 5 and 6) is shown it on their own device before Duel.
10. Given the child has chosen "Turn on mic", when the browser's own question about the microphone is open, then Mic Check tells the child to choose "Allow" ("Say yes to the mic"), the button is busy ("Waiting for the mic…") and "Too noisy? Tap answers instead" is still offered.
11. Given Mic Check is listening, when nothing has been heard after a while, then it says so ("Bolt didn't catch that") and offers "Try again", which listens again, and "Too noisy? Tap answers instead".
12. Given speech recognition fails on the device, when Mic Check is shown, then it says so as an alert ("Bolt's ears aren't working") and "Use number pad" is the only choice it offers besides Back.
13. Given a child chose "Too noisy? Tap answers instead" or switched "Answer by shouting" off, so the number pad is the way of answering kept on the device, when a game is about to begin in a later visit, then Mic Check is skipped and the game starts with the number pad, until "Answer by shouting" is switched on again (docs/adr/0025-answering-by-voice.md).

---

## L2-013: Picking a kid
**Traces to:** L1-004

The five kids, in the order they are offered, with their taglines and descriptions:

- **Zuri** — "First to shout it!" — "Bold and loud. Zuri holds her pencil like a sword and never waits to be asked."
- **Kenji** — "Collects every country" — "The planner. Kenji pins a badge on his backpack for every desk he reaches."
- **Nia** — "Fastest feet in class" — "The sprinter. Nia is always mid-jump and already halfway to the next desk."
- **Milo** — "Quiet. Then BOOM." — "Shy but brilliant. His robot pal Bolt beeps when Milo nails a tricky one."
- **Pip** — "Flies to every desk" — "The dreamer. Pip throws a paper plane ahead to every new country."

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given Pick Your Kid, when it opens, then the five kids (Zuri, Kenji, Nia, Milo and Pip) are offered, each with their colour, and one is selected: the kid kept on the device, or Zuri when none is kept.
2. Given Pick Your Kid, when the child selects a kid, then that kid comes forward with their name, tagline and description, and the confirm button reads "I'm {kid}!".
3. Given a kid is selected, when the child confirms, then the kid and the nickname are kept on the device and Solo Or Friends opens.
4. Given a child has picked a kid before, when Home opens, then it shows their nickname and kid, and "Change kid" opens Pick Your Kid with that kid selected.
5. Given a child who has picked no kid, when Home opens, then no kid, no nickname and no "Change kid" are shown, and Home says "No names, no sign-up. You pick a kid and get a game name."

## L2-014: A generated nickname
**Traces to:** L1-004

A nickname is one adjective and one animal from two fixed lists of ten. The adjectives: Speedy,
Mighty, Clever, Bouncy, Sneaky, Brave, Zippy, Jolly, Cosmic, Turbo. The animals: Otter, Panda,
Falcon, Gecko, Llama, Narwhal, Koala, Fox, Penguin, Yak.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a child with no nickname, when Pick Your Kid opens, then a nickname of one adjective and one animal from fixed lists is generated ("Speedy Otter").
2. Given Pick Your Kid, when the child chooses "New name", then a different nickname is generated from the same lists.
3. Given Pick Your Kid, when it is shown, then there is no field in which a name can be typed.
4. Given a nickname read back from the device that is not one adjective and one animal from the lists, when the game starts, then it is replaced with a newly generated one.

---

## L2-015: What is kept on the device
**Traces to:** L1-004

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a child has picked a kid and a nickname, when they close the browser and open the game again, then Home shows the same kid and nickname.
2. Given the device's storage is unavailable or its content is unreadable, when the game opens, then it starts with the defaults and no error is shown.
3. Given a child has muted sound or changed the game setup, when they return, then the mute and the setup are as they left them.
4. Given the device's storage, when it is inspected, then it holds only the kid, the nickname, the setup, the sound setting and the way of answering ("Answer by shouting" in Settings, L2-032 criterion 4).

## L2-016: Choosing the maths
**Traces to:** L1-005

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given Game Setup opens for the first time, when nothing is changed, then Times is on, tables 2 to 10 are on, and Plus, Minus and Divide are off.
2. Given Game Setup, when the child toggles a kind of maths or a times table, then it turns on or off and its state is exposed as pressed or not pressed.
3. Given only one kind of maths is on, when the child turns it off, then it stays on and Game Setup says why ("Keep at least one kind of maths on. Turn another on first."); and given only one times table is on, when the child turns it off, then it stays on and Game Setup says why ("Keep at least one times table on. Turn another on first.").
4. Given Game Setup, when the child chooses "Easy", then tables 2, 3, 4, 5 and 10 are on; when they choose "All", then tables 2 to 12 are on.
5. Given Times is off, when Game Setup is shown, then the times tables cannot be changed and are not used, "Easy" and "All" are not offered, and Game Setup says why ("Turn on Times to pick tables.").
6. Given Times is turned off and on again, when Game Setup is shown, then the times tables that were on before are on again.

---

## L2-017: Tour length and card speed
**Traces to:** L1-005

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given Game Setup opens for the first time, when nothing is changed, then the tour is 12 desks and the card speed is Normal.
2. Given Game Setup, when the child chooses 6, 12 or 24 desks, then the tour's name and about how long it takes are shown (Quick hop, about 2 minutes; Round the world, about 5; Grand tour, about 10).
3. Given Game Setup, when the child chooses Chill, Normal or Lightning, then the time per card is shown (10, 6 or 3 seconds).

## L2-018: Starting from the setup
**Traces to:** L1-005

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a solo game and Game Setup with no changes, when the child chooses "Go!", then the game starts with the defaults.
2. Given a game with friends, when the host chooses "Save" on Game Setup, then the Lobby shows the chosen maths and tour to every player in the room.
3. Given a game with friends, when a player who is not the host is in the Lobby, then they see the setup and cannot change it.
4. Given a setup sent to the backend, when a value is outside those Game Setup offers, then it is refused with an error `code` and the room's setup is unchanged.
5. Given the host in the Lobby, when they choose "Change" beside the game's setup, then Game Setup opens titled "Set up the race" with "Save" in place of "Go!", and "Save" and Back both return to the Lobby; a player who is not the host is not offered "Change".
6. Given the host has chosen "Save", when the setup is being saved to the room, then the button is busy ("Saving…") and no choice can be changed.
7. Given the room refuses the host's setup or it cannot be saved, when Game Setup shows it, then it says so as an alert ("That setup didn't save", "The room still has the setup from before. Try again."), offers "Try again", and stays open.

---

## L2-019: A solo game is played on the device
**Traces to:** L1-006

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given Solo Or Friends, when the child chooses "Play solo", then a game is set up and played with no request to the backend.
2. Given the device loses its network during a solo game, when the game continues, then duels, hops and the Podium work as before.
3. Given a solo game, when a tour starts, then the four kids the child did not pick are among the classmates and further classmates hold the remaining desks.

## L2-020: Each classmate is its own agent
**Traces to:** L1-006

Each classmate is an agent with a profile: a skill level, characteristics (such as bold, careful
or quick to guess) and preferred kinds of maths. The class is diverse: its profiles differ.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a solo game, when a tour starts, then every classmate has a profile and the class holds at least three different skill levels.
2. Given a classmate of the highest skill level and one of the lowest, when each answers the same 100 flash cards, then the first answers correctly more often and sooner than the second.
3. Given a classmate who prefers times tables, when it answers 100 Times cards and 100 Divide cards, then it answers the Times cards correctly more often and sooner.
4. Given a classmate gives a wrong answer, when the answer is given, then the classmate is frozen for 1 second exactly as a player is (L2-004).
5. Given any classmate and any flash card, when the classmate answers, then no answer arrives sooner than a child could give one (a floor of 800 ms, ADR 0019) and a classmate never wins every duel of a tour of 12 or more desks against a child who answers within 2 seconds.
6. Given one of the five kids is a classmate, when its profile is made, then its characteristics fit that kid (Zuri bold and first to answer, Kenji the planner, Nia the sprinter, Milo quiet and accurate, Pip the dreamer).

---

## L2-021: Classmates driven by the browser's on-device model
**Traces to:** L1-006

Where the browser provides an on-device language model (Gemini Nano through Chrome's built-in
Prompt API), each classmate's agent uses it to decide its answer and when to give it, from its
profile and the flash card. The rules of the duel are never decided by the model.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a browser whose on-device model is ready, when a solo tour starts, then each classmate has its own model session primed with its profile.
2. Given a classmate's model session, when a flash card appears, then the agent's decision is read as structured data (an answer that is a whole number from 0 to 999 and a time to answer), and a decision that is not in that form is discarded.
3. Given the model has not decided by the time the classmate's profile says it would answer, when that time comes, then the classmate answers from its profile without the model, and the flash card is never held back for the model.
4. Given a browser with no on-device model, or one whose model is not downloaded, when a solo tour starts, then every classmate plays from its profile alone, the game starts just as fast, and nothing tells the child that anything is missing.
5. Given the model is not downloaded, when a solo game is played, then the game never starts a download that delays play, and never asks the child to wait for one.
6. Given a solo tour ends or the child leaves it (Home from the Podium or from Pause), when that happens, then every model session is destroyed; the sessions are kept while World Map is shown between the duels of one tour.
7. Given any solo game, when the model is used, then no flash card, profile or model output leaves the device.
8. Given two classmates duel while the child is seated, when World Map shows their duel, then it is worked out from their two profiles alone; the model decides only the duels the child is in (ADR 0030).

## L2-022: Making a room
**Traces to:** L1-007

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given Solo Or Friends, when the child chooses "Make a room", then the backend makes a room with a unique 4-letter code, the child is its host, and the Lobby opens showing the code.
2. Given a room is made, when its code is generated, then it is 4 capital letters from A to Z and is never a word on the list of blocked words.
3. Given the backend cannot be reached, when the child chooses "Make a room", then a message says the room could not be made ("We couldn't make your room", "Something went wrong on our side. Try again, or play solo while you wait.") and offers "Try again", and the child stays on Solo Or Friends.
4. Given one address has made more rooms than the rate limit allows, when it asks for another, then the request is refused with status 429, Solo Or Friends tells the child to wait ("Whoa, slow down a moment", "That's a lot of rooms! Wait a little, then try again.") and "Make a room" is disabled.
5. Given a room is being made, when Solo Or Friends is shown, then "Make a room" is busy and reads "Making…", and "Join a room" is disabled.
6. Given the device has no network, when Solo Or Friends is shown, then a banner says so ("You're offline. You can still play solo."), "Make a room" and "Join a room" are disabled ("Playing with friends needs the internet.") and "Play solo" works (L2-054 criterion 3).

## L2-023: Joining a room with its code
**Traces to:** L1-007

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given Room, when the child types, then only letters are accepted, they are shown as capitals in four boxes, and no more than four are taken.
2. Given fewer than four letters, when Room is shown, then "Join" is disabled.
3. Given a code for a room that is waiting and has fewer than 8 players, when the child chooses "Join", then they join the room and the Lobby opens.
4. Given a code for no room, when the child chooses "Join", then Room says "Hmm, no room with that code. Check the letters?" as an alert and the code can be changed.
5. Given a code for a room with 8 players, when the child chooses "Join", then Room says the room is full ("That room is full (8 kids max).").
6. Given a code for a room whose game has started, when the child chooses "Join", then Room says that game has already started ("That game already started. Ask for the next one!").
7. Given a join is in progress, when Room is shown, then "Join" is disabled and reads "Joining…", and Room says "Finding your friends…".
8. Given a code typed in lower case, when the child joins, then it matches the room whose code is the same letters in capitals.
9. Given the backend cannot be reached, when the child chooses "Join", then Room says so as an alert ("We can't reach the game right now. Check the internet and try again."), the code can be sent again, and "Play solo instead" opens Game Setup.

## L2-024: The Lobby
**Traces to:** L1-007

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given the Lobby, when it is shown, then it shows the code in the largest type on the screen, the players in the room each with their nickname and kid, the host marked "Host", the device's own player marked "(you)", the count out of 8 and the empty desks.
2. Given a player joins or leaves the room, when it happens, then every Lobby in the room shows the change within 1 second without being reloaded.
3. Given two players in a room pick the same kid, when the Lobby is shown, then both are shown as that kid and are told apart by their nicknames.
4. Given the Lobby, when the child chooses to copy the room's link, then the link is copied, a message says so ("Link copied", "Send it to a friend to get them in."), and opening it on another device opens Room with the code filled in; when the link cannot be copied, then a message says to say the code out loud instead ("Couldn't copy the link") and stays until it is dismissed.
5. Given the Lobby, when it is shown, then it shows the game's setup: the maths, the tour length ("{n} desks") and how answers are given ("Answer by: Shouting it!"). How answers are given is not a value of the room's setup: each player chooses voice or the number pad on their own device (L2-011, L2-012, L2-032 criterion 4). What the line reads for a player who answers with the number pad is **to confirm**.
6. Given a player joins the room, when it happens, then every other Lobby in the room says so once in a passing message ("{nickname} joined", "as {kid}") that leaves by itself after 4 seconds; messages stack with the newest at the bottom and each can be dismissed.

## L2-025: The host starts the game
**Traces to:** L1-007

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given the host is alone in the room, when the Lobby is shown, then "Start the race" is disabled and the Lobby says it is waiting for at least one friend ("Waiting for at least one friend…").
2. Given at least two players in the room, when the host chooses "Start the race", then the game starts for every player in the room; until then the host's Lobby says how many are in ("{n} kids ready").
3. Given a player who is not the host, when the Lobby is shown, then there is no "Start the race" and the Lobby says who they are waiting for ("You're in!", "Waiting for {host's nickname} to start…").
4. Given a player who is not the host, when they send a request to start the game or change its setup, then the backend refuses it with an error `code`.
5. Given the host leaves the room before the game starts, when they leave, then the player who joined earliest becomes the host and is told so ("You're the host now", "The host left. You can start the race."); and when the last player leaves, the room is gone.

---

## L2-026: The server decides a game with friends
**Traces to:** L1-008

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a duel in a room, when the traveller's and the desk holder's correct answers both reach the server, then the one the server received first wins, whatever time either client claims.
2. Given a duel in a room, when a client sends a message claiming it won, hopped or ended the tour, then the server ignores the claim and the game is unchanged.
3. Given a duel in a room, when an answer arrives, then the server checks it against the flash card it made, and applies the freeze, the hop and the end of the tour itself.
4. Given a player is frozen on the server, when their next answer arrives within 1 second, then it is ignored even if the client did not show the freeze.
5. Given a flash card in a room, when it is sent to the players, then both players in the duel are sent it in the same server step and the message does not hold the answer.

---

## L2-027: Every player sees the same game
**Traces to:** L1-008

## L2-052: Real time through Azure SignalR Service
**Traces to:** L1-017

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given the deployed backend, when a player connects, then the SignalR connection is served through Azure SignalR Service, by WebSockets.
2. Given two instances of the backend, when players of one room are connected to different instances, then each sees the same game.
3. Given a local or test run with no Azure SignalR Service set, when the backend starts, then it serves SignalR itself and every test passes.
4. Given the settings a deployed system needs (the Azure SignalR Service connection, the frontend's origin, the rate limits), when the backend is deployed, then each is documented and none is in the source.

## L2-053: Telemetry without anything about a child
**Traces to:** L1-017

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given the backend, when it handles a request or a hub message, then a trace and metrics are emitted through OpenTelemetry, with the route recorded as its template.
2. Given every log, metric and span, when it is inspected, then it holds no nickname, room code, connection token, message body or query string.
3. Given the backend, when its health is asked for, then it answers whether it is live and whether it is ready.

## L2-054: Failures a screen can explain
**Traces to:** L1-017

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given the backend refuses a request, when the error is returned, then it is a ProblemDetails with a `code`, and the screen shows a message for that code in words a child understands.
2. Given an error the screen has no message for, when it is shown, then a generic message is shown and the detail from the backend is not.
3. Given the backend cannot be reached, when a child is on Solo Or Friends, then "Play solo" still works.
4. Given the game itself breaks, when the failure is caught, then a screen says so in plain words ("Oops, the game tripped over", "That wasn't you. Try again, and if it keeps happening, tell a grown-up.") and offers "Try again", which loads the game afresh at the address where it broke, and "Home"; nothing of the failure is shown.
